Worked example

The read-through that would have cost you.

In April 2025 Siemens' energy business demerged into a separately listed company. Since then the energy names have printed extraordinary numbers:

Siemens Energy India +68% Hitachi Energy +1,160% GE Vernova T&D +115%

Every one is a grid business. None of it accrues to SIEMENS.NS any more. Read the sector and you conclude Siemens is compounding.

How the desk handled it
  1. 1News analystFlags Siemens Energy India as a separate entity with “recurring misattribution risk”.
  2. 2FundamentalsFiles the same fact under a heading it labels “Demerger context (critical)”.
  3. 3BullUses the demerger to excuse depressed earnings — then borrows the peer growth anyway.
  4. 4BearCatches exactly that: you cannot both discount the earnings and claim the peers.
  5. 5Research managerRules the point decisive.
  6. 6Conservative & neutralIndependently catch the aggressive analyst trying the same move. “Tries to have it both ways.”
  7. 7Final callUnderweight, and it cites the demerger as a reason.

Not a rating — seven passes of somebody checking the story before it reaches you.

Where we draw the line

Three things it will not do.

It won't invent a number.

If there is no honest figure for a field, the field stays empty and says why. Every number that does appear carries the sentence it was taken from.

It won't hide a change of mind.

Run the same stock twice and the rating can come back different, because the evidence genuinely is balanced. You are told, rather than shown whichever answer arrived last.

It won't tell you to buy.

This is research, not advice. It has no idea what else you hold or when you need the money. It hands you the argument. The decision stays yours.

Before you subscribe

The questions worth asking.

Is this investment advice?
No. TickerInvest is a research tool. It reads public data, argues both sides of a stock, and shows its working. It knows nothing about your holdings, income, tax position or timeline, so it cannot and does not tell you what to do with your money.
How accurate is it?
Wrong question, and it is the one every rating service dodges. A single call is a probability, not a fact — anyone quoting you a hit rate is selling. What we can give you is where every claim came from, so you judge the argument instead of trusting the score. When the evidence is genuinely balanced, the run says so rather than manufacturing conviction.
Where do the numbers come from?
Published financial statements, NSE and BSE filings and announcements, price and volume data, Indian and global news coverage, and public retail forums. Each analyst pulls its own data before it is allowed to reason, so it argues from what it fetched rather than what it remembers.
Can it make things up?
Language models can. This one is built so that when it tries, you see it: every extracted figure is shown with the sentence it was taken from, and any field without an honest source is left visibly empty instead of filled. If a report and the statements disagree, the analyst has to say which it trusts and why — there is a real example of that in the run on this page.
Which stocks can I run?
Anything listed on the NSE or BSE. Type the bare ticker and it resolves to the exchange for you. Coverage of smaller names is limited by what is published about them — where the data is thin, the run tells you it is thin.
Why does it take four minutes?
Because twelve agents are reading real documents rather than looking up a cached score. Four analysts run at once, then the debate has to happen in order — the bear cannot answer an argument the bull has not made yet. Leave the tab; the run finishes without you, and reports become readable as each analyst lands.
What if I stop finding it useful?
Cancel from the account page in two clicks and you keep access to the end of the period you paid for. Your past runs stay downloadable. Start on the free plan — two full-depth runs is enough to tell whether the argument is worth reading.
